# Boards

A board is a workspace: one project, one team, one list of things to do. It holds
areas, and the areas hold cards. Everything else in LokalBoards hangs off a
board.

![The dashboard, showing four boards with their menus](/images/docs/dashboard.webp)

The dashboard lists every board you own or have been invited to. Boards you were
invited to carry a **Shared** badge, and the avatars on a tile are the people who
can see it.

Every tile carries a **⋮** of its own, holding the same things the board's own
page offers: its settings, who is on it, and deleting it — or, for a board
somebody shared with you, leaving it. Renaming a board or inviting somebody to it
does not need the board to be opened first.

![A board tile's menu, with settings, invite, duplicate and archive](/images/docs/board-tile-menu.webp)

## Creating a board

Click the blue **+** at the top right. Give the board a name and save — you can
change everything else later.

A section with nothing in it — a dashboard with no boards yet, or a group you
have just made — shows a **+** tile of its own instead, and a board made from
that one lands in that section.

![The dialog for a new board, with its name, thumbnail, colour, style and privacy](/images/docs/board-create.webp)

The **⋮** button beside it holds **Import boards**, for bringing boards over from
Trello, Wekan or Nextcloud Deck — see
[Bringing boards over](#bringing-boards-over).

Use **New group** at the bottom of the dashboard to sort boards into groups once
you have more than a screenful.

## Board options

Click the **⋮** on the board's tile, or open the board and click the **⋮** at the
top right. Either reaches the same options.

![The board options dialog: name, thumbnail, colour, style and status](/images/docs/board-options.webp)

**Thumbnail and colour** decide how the board's tile looks on the dashboard.
Pick one of the supplied images or upload your own, or pick one of the twelve
colours — or mix your own with the pipette. A tile shows one or the other:
choosing a colour clears the image, and choosing an image clears the colour.
Whichever you pick, the board's name, the avatars and the badges stay readable,
because the tile works out whether it needs light or dark text for the colour you
chose.

**Style** switches between **KanBan**, where areas stand side by side as columns,
and **ToDo**, where they stack full width as lists. It is the same board and the
same cards either way — see [Areas](/docs/areas) for when each one helps.

**Status** decides who can reach the board:

- **Private** — only you and the people you invite.
- **Public** — anyone with the link can open it.

Everyone looking at the board sees the change as it is saved, and so does every
dashboard that has a tile for it — a renamed board is renamed everywhere, and a
new colour arrives on the tile without anybody reloading.

## Sharing a board

Click **⋮ › Permissions** on a board to see who has access and to add someone.

![The permissions dialog, listing two members and the field for adding another](/images/docs/board-invite.webp)

Search for the person by name or e-mail address. If they already have an account
here, pick them from the list. If they do not, type their full e-mail address:
LokalBoards sends them an invitation to create an account, and signing up through
that link joins them to this board with the access you chose. The link works once
and expires after 14 days, and it carries the address it was sent to — nobody can
use it to register under a different one.

An invitation works even on an instance with public signup switched off, which is
the point: the board owner decides who joins, not the sign-up form.

Choose what they may do:

- **Read only** — they can open the board and follow it, but change nothing.
- **Read & write** — they can move cards, write comments and edit anything on it.

The list at the top shows everyone who already has access, with an eye or a
pencil for their permission. Use the pencil to change it and the bin to take it
away again.

## Everyone sees it at once

Boards are live. When somebody moves a card, renames an area, ticks something off
or writes a comment, everyone looking at that board sees it happen — no refresh,
no stale screen, and no two people quietly editing different versions of the same
thing.

If you are not at your desk when it happens, an e-mail goes out once an hour with
everything that changed on the boards you are on.

## Duplicating a board

**⋮ › Duplicate board**, on the board or on its tile on the dashboard, makes a
new board with the same columns and none of the cards. Give it a name — the
original's is offered, selected, so typing replaces it — and the copy opens
ready to fill in.

![Naming the copy before it is made](/images/docs/board-duplicate.webp)

Most boards start the same way, with the same three or four columns, and this is
the part of setting one up that is worth skipping. The copy keeps the style and
the colour, is yours whatever the original was, and starts private. Archived
columns are left behind, being no longer part of the board's shape.

## Exporting a board

**⋮ › Export board** downloads the board as a zip. Inside is one JSON file
holding every area and every card — descriptions, labels, due dates, the people
on each, reminders, comments and history — and an `attachments` folder with
the files the cards hold, pictures pasted into descriptions and comments
included. Archived areas and cards are in it too, each with the date it was
archived.

Anybody who can see a board can export it. Everything in the file is something
they could already open card by card, and the export only saves them the
clicking. People appear by name; email addresses are not in it.

The JSON is meant to be read — by a script, by another tool, or by you in a few
years. Every path in it is relative to the zip, so an attachment's `file` can be
followed as it stands. The format is described under
[Export](/api/export#the-board-file).

### As a spreadsheet

**⋮ › Export as spreadsheet** downloads the board's cards as a CSV file for
Excel, Numbers or LibreOffice — one row per card, in the order the board shows
them: its area, title, status, due date and how it repeats, the people on it,
its labels, checklist progress, comment and attachment counts, the description,
and a link back to the card. Archived cards are left out; the file is the board
as it stands.

It opens with a double-click. The headings are in the instance's language, and
so is the separator: a comma on an English instance, a semicolon on the others,
which is what a spreadsheet in those languages expects. A title that starts
like a formula — with `=`, `+`, `-` or `@` — is written as text, so opening the
file never runs anything.

To export every board at once, together with the database, see
[Exporting everything](/docs/users#exporting-everything).

## Bringing boards over

**⋮ › Import boards** on the dashboard recreates boards from another tool as
private boards of yours. What arrives: the lists as areas, the cards in order
with their descriptions, checklists, labels, due dates and whether they were
done, and the comments, each with the name of whoever wrote it. The people
themselves are not carried over — nobody on the other tool has an account here —
and neither are archived lists or cards.

- **Trello, a public board:** paste its link.
- **Trello, a private board:** in Trello, open the board's menu and choose
  *Print, export and share → Export as JSON*, then choose that file. Trello's
  export links to a card's files rather than carrying them, and for a private
  board only somebody signed in to Trello can open those links. You are, so
  the dialog asks you to lend the import that for a moment: **Allow in
  Trello** opens Trello's own page asking whether LokalBoards may read your
  boards for an hour; allow it, paste the token Trello then shows, and choose
  **Import with the files**. The token is used for those downloads and kept
  nowhere, and it expires on its own an hour later. Choose **Import without
  the files** instead and each card lists its files as links under its
  description, to download by hand while the board is still in Trello.

  Either way, pictures pasted into a description or a comment are stored here
  like pictures pasted into a LokalBoards card, and the text points at them —
  nothing on the board keeps depending on Trello. Trello's export holds only
  the 1,000 most recent actions, so on a busy board the oldest comments are not
  in it.

  The step with the token needs a Trello API key on the instance — see
  [below](#a-trello-api-key). Without one, a private board imports with its
  files as links.
- **Wekan:** in the board's menu, *Export board → JSON*, then choose that file.
  The file carries the board's attachments inside it, and they come along too.
  A Wekan card counts as done when its due date was marked complete. Swimlanes
  have no counterpart here, so every card goes into its list whichever
  swimlane it was in.
- **Nextcloud Deck:** Deck has no export button. An administrator runs
  `occ deck:export <user>` on the Nextcloud server, which writes every board
  that user owns into one file; choose that file and each board comes across as
  a board of its own. Deck leaves attachments out of that file, so they cannot
  come along.

Which tool a file came from is read from the file, so there is nothing to pick.

### A Trello API key

Trello hands out tokens to apps it knows by an API key. It takes a few minutes
to get one, once per instance:

1. Open [trello.com/apps/admin](https://trello.com/apps/admin), choose
   **New**, and create a Power-Up — call it after your instance; it only ever
   exists to issue the key, and is never added to a board.
2. In the Power-Up, open the **Trello Auth** tab and choose **Generate a new API
   Key**.
3. Put it in the instance's configuration and restart:
   `NUXT_PUBLIC_TRELLO_API_KEY=<the key>`

The key is not a secret — it only names the app asking for a token, and it is
in the page's source — so it needs no more care than the instance's address.
The tokens are the secret, and LokalBoards never keeps one.
The importers are tested against real exports from Wekan (September 2026) and
from Nextcloud Deck 1.19; a file from a much older version may be refused
rather than half read.

## Filtering what you see

The funnel beside the **⋮** narrows the board to the cards you want to look at:
by label, by who it is assigned to, by whether it is overdue or due today or due
this week or has no date at all, and by open or done. Picking two labels widens
the answer — cards wearing either one — while picking a label *and* an assignee
narrows it.

![The filter menu, with a label picked and the counts following it](/images/docs/board-filter.webp)

Each area's header counts its cards, and while a filter is on it counts both:
**3 / 12** means three of its twelve cards match. Nothing is moved or changed;
cards you filtered out are simply not drawn, and **Clear filters** brings them
back.

The filter is kept in the address, so a filtered board is a link. Copy it from
the address bar and send it, and whoever opens it sees the board filtered the
same way — as long as they can see the board at all. Picking and unpicking does
not pile up in the browser's history, so **Back** still takes you off the board
rather than through every chip you tried.

If you would rather write the link than click it together:

| Parameter | Values |
|-----------|--------|
| `labels` | Label ids, separated by commas. |
| `assignee` | Account ids, separated by commas, or `none` for cards nobody is on. A card on several people shows for any one of them. |
| `due` | `overdue`, `today`, `week` or `none`. |
| `status` | `open` or `done`. |

A value the board does not recognise is ignored rather than refused, so a link
with one typo in it still opens the board, filtered by the rest.

## Archiving a board

**⋮ › Archive board**, then confirm. The board leaves your dashboard and
everybody else's, keeping its areas, cards, comments and attachments exactly as
they are.

![The confirmation asked for before a board is archived](/images/docs/board-archive.webp)

Nothing is destroyed. **⋮ › Recently archived** on the dashboard lists what you have
archived and puts it back. An archived board still opens from a link or a
bookmark — it says at the top that it is archived, and its owner can restore it
from there.

The same applies inside a board: the button on an area's header archives the
area with the cards in it, and **Archive card** on an open card archives that
one. Both are listed in **⋮ › Recently archived** on the board, with the date they were
put away, and both come back exactly as they were.

![The archive, listing what a board has put away](/images/docs/archive.webp)

Deleting something outright is still possible, and it lives in the archive: the
bin beside an entry removes it and everything belonging to it for good. That one
cannot be undone, which is why it is the only place in the app you will find it.

**The archive empties itself after 30 days.** It says so at the top, and the
clock starts when a thing was archived rather than when it was made. Thirty days
is long enough that a mistake is recoverable well after anybody notices it, and
short enough that an instance does not quietly fill with work somebody threw
away last year — a card that goes takes its attachments and their files with it.

Whoever runs the instance can change that with `NUXT_ARCHIVE_RETENTION_DAYS` —
`90` for a quarter, say. **Set it to `0` to keep archived things for ever**,
which is how the archive behaved before v0.37.0. Anything archived under an
earlier version is subject to the window from the moment this one runs, dated
from when it was archived.
