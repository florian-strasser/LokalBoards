# Cards

A card is one piece of work. It lives in an area, and everything about it — the
description, who is doing it, when it is due, what has been said about it and
whatever files belong to it — is on the card itself.

## Creating a card

Click **Create new card** at the bottom of an area, type a title and save. That
is enough; a card with nothing but a title is a perfectly good card.

## Opening a card

Click it. Everything else happens in the card itself.

![An open card, with its due date, assignee, description, checklist, attachments and comments](/images/docs/card.webp)

## The description

The description takes **Markdown**, so it holds more than a paragraph: headings,
bold and italic, bullet lists, links — and checklists.

```markdown
Refresh the brand mark for the 2.0 launch. Keep it **simple** and _legible_ at
small sizes.

### Requirements

- Works on light and dark backgrounds
- Scales down to a 16px favicon

- [x] Collect references
- [ ] First round of concepts
- [ ] Team review
```

A `- [ ]` line becomes a checkbox you can tick, and the card counts them for you:
the card's tile on the board shows **1/3** and keeps the number up to date as the
boxes are ticked. There is nothing to switch on — it is the ordinary Markdown you
already wrote.

## Due dates and who is doing it

Set a **due date** and it appears on the card's tile, so the board shows what is
coming without anybody opening anything. Assign the card to somebody and their
avatar appears on it.

## Labels

Give a card **labels** — a short word like *Bug*, *Blocked* or *Design* — and
they appear on the card under its buttons and on its tile, so a board says what
kind of work it is holding at a glance.

**Labels**, in the card's row of buttons, adds one. Type the word; anything the
board is already using is offered under the field, and picking it there is the
same as typing it out — the same word is the same label, so a board never ends
up with three spellings of *Bug*.

The labels themselves sit on the line under those buttons, and that is where you
change them: click the word to reword it, or the cross at its end to take it
off. Both act on the card you are on — rewording points *that* card at a
different word and leaves every other card alone. A word no card uses any more
stops being offered.

A label is one of the words a card can be found by: searching for *Bug* turns up
the cards marked *Bug*, alongside the ones whose name or description mentions
it, and each hit shows its labels.

Adding, rewording or removing a label is written into the card's history, under
**Comments and activity**, and everybody else looking at the board sees the
change as it happens.

Every label is drawn in the same colour, so a board reads by what its labels say
rather than by a code you have to remember. Adding or changing one needs
permission to edit the board; anyone who can see the board sees the labels.

## Attachments

Add files to a card with **Add attachment(s)**. They are stored on your own
server, alongside everything else.

Images open in a lightbox when clicked, so a screenshot attached to a bug report
is one click from being readable:

![An attached image opened in the lightbox](/images/docs/card-lightbox.webp)

Anything that is not an image or a PDF downloads instead of trying to open in the
browser — a spreadsheet is a file you want on your disk, not a tab full of
characters.

## Marking a card done

The circle beside the card's title is its status. Tick it and the circle fills;
the card reads as finished wherever it appears, whichever area it happens to be
sitting in.

## Moving a card

Drag a card to another area or another position to move it — it saves as you drop
it.

## Duplicating and deleting

The **⋮** button at the top of an open card holds both:

![The card menu, with duplicate and delete](/images/docs/card-menu.webp)

**Duplicate card** makes a second copy directly underneath the original, with the
title, the description and its checklist, the due date and its reminders, the
assignee, the done state and the attachments. Comments are not copied — they are
a conversation about one card, and they stay on the card they were written on.
Attached files are copied properly rather than shared, so deleting the copy's
attachment never touches the original's file.

It is the quickest way to work from a card as a template: build the checklist
once, then duplicate it for each new job and change what differs.

**Delete card** removes it, with its comments and attachments, after a
confirmation.

## What the tile tells you

The tile on the board is a summary of everything above, so you rarely have to
open a card to know where it stands:

- the status circle, filled when it is done
- **1/3** — ticked checklist items out of the total
- a comment count and an attachment count
- the due date
- the avatar of whoever it is assigned to, and of anyone reading it right now
